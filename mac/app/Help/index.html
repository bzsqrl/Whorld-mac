Whorld for Mac

A native macOS port of Whorld, the free sacred-geometry visualizer by Chris Korda.

Whorld draws animated rings of polygons and stars. You change how they grow, spin, skew and color with the parameters, and play them live with the keyboard or a MIDI controller. The Mac version is based on Whorld V2 and draws a patch the same way the Windows version does.

The detailed documentation for each parameter and feature is the Whorld manual. It was written for Whorld V1 on Windows, so some of what it says about menus, dialogs and installation differs from this app; this page lists the differences.

Files

Patches (.whp), playlists (.whl), snapshots (.whs) and movies (.whm) are the same files Windows Whorld uses, and can be moved between the two in either direction. Double-click a patch or playlist in the Finder to open it, or choose it from File > Open Recent. When you quit, Whorld remembers the playlist and patch you had open (the patch file, or the playlist entry that was playing) and opens them again next time; an untitled patch or a playlist that was never saved starts fresh, and a file that has since been moved is still found. To start with an untitled patch instead, as Windows Whorld does, turn off Whorld > Settings > General > Reopen the last playlist and patch at launch. Playing a patch from the playlist (or by MIDI or OSC) replaces the one showing without asking to save it, so you can move between patches freely while performing; to keep changes, save them first (⌘S). File > New, File > Open and Quit still ask. A patch can name its author: the window's title bar and the top of the Properties panel show the playing patch's name and author, and the Playlist has an Author column. Set the author in the Save As dialog's Author field, or with File > Patch Author… (then save). For a patch that has no author yet, both suggest Whorld > Settings > General > Your name. The author is a line of its own in the patch file, which Windows Whorld ignores (and leaves out if it saves the patch again).

Windows and panels

As on Windows, the Whorld window holds the picture in the middle and the panels around it: Parameters, Master, Globals, Properties, Playlist and MIDI Mapping. Show or hide each from the View menu (shortcuts below). The panels sit in a left, a right and a bottom area; drag the dividers between them to resize. To move a panel, drag its title bar onto another panel: near one of that panel's edges, it goes above, below, left or right of it; on its title bar or in its middle, the two are stacked as tabs, as on Windows (what will happen is highlighted while you drag). Click a tab to show its panel, and drag a tab to move that panel out of the stack. A panel's View menu shortcut brings it to the front when it's a tab behind another. Or drop it near the left, right or bottom edge of the picture to add it to that area, or use the ⋯ menu on its title bar, which can also float it in a window of its own (its Dock button puts it back). Window > Show All Panels shows every panel and fits the Whorld window to its screen; Window > Reset Layout puts everything back in its standard place: Parameters on the left beside the picture, and Playlist, Properties, Master and Globals along the bottom. The arrangement is remembered between launches. To keep an arrangement for later, choose Window > Save Layout…, which saves the panels, their sizes and the windows in a .whlayout file; Window > Open Layout… (or double-clicking the file) brings it back, adapted to the screen. A playlist can carry a layout too: tick Include the panel layout in the Save Playlist As dialog, and opening the playlist arranges the panels that way (turn this off in Whorld > Settings > General > Open playlists with their layout). Windows Whorld ignores a playlist's layout. In the panels, click a slider and use ← → to move it in fine steps, ⇧← ⇧→ for larger steps, and ⇧⌘← ⇧⌘→ to jump to either end; in a number box, ↑ ↓ do the same (fine, with ⇧ larger, with ⇧⌘ to the end of the range, where the range has one). Right-click (or Control-click) a slider or number box to set it back to its default. On a trackpad, rest the pointer on a slider and swipe sideways with two fingers to move it, or on a number box and swipe up or down to step it (with ⇧ held, in larger steps). A swipe that starts anywhere else scrolls the panel as usual. In Properties, click a row, then ↑ ↓ move through the list and Space toggles the row (elsewhere, Space pauses). The options dialog is Whorld > Settings (⌘,).

Window > Detach (⇧⌘D) moves the picture into a window of its own, as on Windows, and the panels then fill the Whorld window. Detach again, or close the picture's window, to put it back.

Full screen and projectors

View > Enter Full Screen (⇧⌘F) fills the display the Whorld window is on; ⇧⌘F or Esc returns to the window. F11, the Windows shortcut, works too where the keyboard sends it to apps; on most Mac keyboards F11 is a media key, and fn-F11 shows the desktop. To show Whorld on a projector or second display, choose Window > Detach, drag the picture's window onto that display, then press ⇧⌘F. On a second display the menu bar and Dock stay on your main display, so the panels remain usable there while the picture fills the projector. On the main display, full screen hides the menu bar and Dock (they don't slide in; press ⇧⌘F or Esc to leave). If the second display is disconnected, Whorld leaves full screen. In full screen the pointer is hidden while it's over the picture. Hold the mouse button (or press the trackpad) on the picture and its center follows the mouse; let go and it stays there (Origin Motion becomes Park, and Edit > Undo puts it back). Leaving full screen puts the origin back in the middle (Origin Center). The renderer is set in Settings > Rendering: Metal (the GPU, the default) or Core Graphics. There too, Frame rate can hold the picture to 60 fps: rings move a step each frame, so on a 120 Hz display patches otherwise run twice as fast as on a 60 Hz monitor, and 60 fps uses less power.

Syphon

Syphon output sends Whorld's picture to other apps on the same Mac, such as Resolume, MadMapper, OBS and TouchDesigner, where it's listed as Whorld. Turn it on with View > Syphon Output or in Whorld > Settings > Syphon (it's off until you do). Transparency comes through: where a patch's Bk Alpha or Alpha is below 1, the receiving app can layer Whorld over other material. By default Syphon carries the frames Whorld shows, at their size (the window's, or the display's in full screen; on a Retina display, View > Full Resolution off halves it). Settings > Syphon > Size > Fixed sends a size of your choosing instead, such as 1920 × 1080, whatever the window's shape: each frame is drawn a second time at that size, with the picture scaled to fit and more of the pattern on the longer side. The fixed-size frame is drawn only while an app is receiving it. Settings shows whether an app is receiving.

OSC and TouchOSC

Everything MIDI can map can also be controlled by OSC, over the network. Whorld listens on UDP port 8000 and answers on port 8001 (Whorld > Settings > Control, which also shows this Mac's address). It sends its state to every device that has sent it something in the last 30 seconds, and to "Also send to" if set: the values of the controls below as they change, and once a second the frame statistics. macOS may ask whether Whorld can use the local network: allow it, or other devices can't reach it.

TouchOSC: the DMG's TouchOSC folder has Whorld.tosc, a layout for a tablet or a computer with four pages: MAIN (Master, the Image switches and actions, the origin), PARAMS, GLOBALS and MONITOR. In TouchOSC set a connection to UDP, host = the Whorld Mac's address (127.0.0.1 on the same Mac), send port 8000, receive port 8001; open the layout and start it (▶). It shows "connected" when Whorld answers.

Addresses start with /w. Numbers are what a MIDI mapping sends: 0 to 1 covers a slider's range (values beyond act like a mapping with a wider Start and End). Switches take 1 or 0 (no argument toggles); actions happen on a message with no argument or a nonzero one.

MIDI

Whorld listens to MIDI input through Core MIDI. Choose the input device in Settings. To map a control, open MIDI Mapping, select one or more mappings, turn on MIDI Learn (⇧⌘L) and move the control on your device.

Movies and video

Movies record every frame's geometry, so they can be replayed and exported at any size. Besides exporting an image sequence, the Mac version can export a movie straight to a QuickTime video (Movie > Export Video…) as H.264, HEVC, ProRes 422 or ProRes 4444. ProRes 4444 keeps the colors nearly exactly; the other formats soften color at sharp edges.

Hue, Rotate Hue and Invert Color

These three color controls from Whorld 1 work again in Whorld for Mac (Windows Whorld V2 keeps the settings in patches but doesn't use them). Hue (Master pane, 0 to 360 degrees) shifts the color of new rings; existing rings keep theirs, so a change sweeps outward as they're replaced. With Loop Hue on, it shifts the range of colors being looped through. Image > Rotate Hue (F3) turns the colors of the rings already showing by 90 degrees and adds 90 to Hue, so new rings follow; four in a row come back to where you started. Undo puts Hue back, but not the colors already turned. Image > Invert Color (F4, or Properties) inverts the colors of all rings, new and existing, as a photographic negative: hue and lightness both flip, so while it's on, raising Lightness darkens the rings. The background isn't affected. All three are saved with the patch and can be MIDI mapping targets or set by OSC. Normally a patch's colors carry on from where the previous patch left them, as on Windows, so the same patch can look different each time; to have each patch start from its own Hue, choose Whorld > Settings > Rendering > Patch colors > Start from the patch.

Fading rings

Normally, once there are as many rings as Master > Rings allows, the oldest ring vanishes each time a new one appears. Master > Fade makes them fade out instead: at 20, for example, each ring fades smoothly from its own transparency to nothing over its last 20 rings before it would vanish. Fade goes from 0 (off, as in Windows Whorld) to 200, is saved with the patch, and can be a MIDI mapping target. Whorld > Settings > Rendering > Ring fade curve chooses between Linear, an even fade, and Eased, which starts and ends gently. The fade applies to the picture, snapshots, movies and exports; snapshots and movies don't record the Rings setting, so their own ring count stands in for it. With Rings at its maximum (unlimited), rings only disappear once they've left the picture, so nothing fades. Patches with Fade above 0 still open in Windows Whorld, which ignores Fade.

Updates

Once a day Whorld asks GitHub whether a newer version has been released (only Whorld's version number is sent). If there is one, it shows what's new, with a button that opens the download page; install it as before, by dragging Whorld from the DMG to Applications. If you're running a beta, you're offered new betas too. Nothing is announced while Whorld is full screen; you'll hear about it when you leave. Choose Whorld > Check for Updates to check now, or turn off automatic checks in Whorld > Settings > General.

Bugs and ideas

Help > Report a Bug and Help > Request a Feature open a form on GitHub, with your Whorld version, macOS version and Mac filled in. You need a free GitHub account to send it. Requests from others are listed on the feature requests page; a 👍 on one helps decide what comes next.

First launch

This build is not notarized by Apple, so the first time you open it macOS says it can't check the app for malicious software. Control-click Whorld in the Applications folder, choose Open, then Open again. On macOS 15 and later, if there is no Open button, go to System Settings > Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. You only need to do this once.
