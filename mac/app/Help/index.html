Whorld for Mac

A native macOS port of Whorld, the free sacred-geometry visualizer by Chris Korda.

Whorld draws animated rings of polygons and stars. You change how they grow, spin, skew and color with the parameters, and play them live with the keyboard, a trackpad or a MIDI controller. The Mac version is based on Whorld V2 and draws a patch the same way the Windows version does.

The detailed documentation for each parameter is the Whorld manual. It was written for Whorld V1 on Windows, so what it says about menus, dialogs and installation differs from this app; this page covers the Mac. For the shortcuts and gestures that are easy to miss, see Tips and Gestures.

Getting started

  1. Open a playlist. Choose File > Playlist > Open Playlist (⇧⌘O). Whorld keeps playlists in Documents > Whorld > Playlists. Or browse patches one by one in View > Patches.
  2. Play a patch. Double-click one in the Playlist panel. ⌘↓ and ⌘↑ play the next and previous patch, from anywhere.
  3. Swipe over the picture. A two-finger swipe up or down (or the mouse wheel) zooms; with ⇧ it changes Damping.
  4. Hold a key and swipe. Each letter moves one control: hold Q over the picture and swipe to change Ring Spacing, W for Line Width. Press ` (left of 1) to see them all on a keyboard, and again to hide it. The Perform Bar names the control as soon as you press the key. View > Key Map (⌥⌘9) lists them all.
  5. Go full screen. ⌘F fills the display the picture is on; ⇧⌘F fills another display, such as a projector. Esc leaves.

Also handy from the start: Space pauses, ⌘B fades to black and back, and View > Show Perform Bar (⇧⌘B) puts the frame rate and the live controls under the picture.

Playing live

The Perform Bar

View > Show Perform Bar (⇧⌘B) adds a thin bar along the bottom of the picture, never over it (on the swipe pad while the picture is detached). Its buttons act on the first click, even while another Whorld window is in front. From the left:

  • The eye: Blackout (⌘B), red while the picture is faded out.
  • Frames a second: orange when display refreshes were missed in the last second. Click it for the rest: refreshes missed, the time to draw a frame on the CPU and GPU, the rings drawn, the display's rate, the renderer, and what Automatic quality has lowered. A click elsewhere closes it.
  • The last control changed, with its value: in a panel or a menu, by MIDI or OSC, or by swiping over the picture (not by modulation). Column names are shortened, such as Ring Growth Amp; the full name is in its tooltip. A Key Map key shows its control here as soon as you press it.
  • The playlist place (12/86; the patch's name is in its tooltip). A click plays the next patch.
  • Play / Stop: Auto Advance (⌥⌘L). The line under Stop fills toward the next patch. Right-click Play for a length.
  • Full or Geom: the patch mode (Image > Geometry Only).
  • Auto: Automatic quality, on (bright) or off (dim) (⌘;). Orange as Auto ↓ while it has lowered something; its tooltip says what.
  • Res: the Render quality menu, titled with the resolution drawn (Res 100%), orange while Automatic quality draws lower than your setting.
  • Mod: audio modulation on or off (⌘'). Its dot is faint while off, hollow while Whorld is finding the beat, filled once locked, and flashes on each beat. Click the tempo beside it to reset to 1.1 (the beat nearest now becomes bar 1, beat 1).
  • Syp: Syphon on or off. The green dot is faint while off, hollow while no app is receiving, filled once one is.
  • At the right end: Detach, lit while the picture is in its own window (a click docks it), and full screen (Option-click either for another display).

Playlists

  • Double-click a patch, or press ⌘↓ / ⌘↑ (Image > Next Patch and Previous Patch), to play it. These keys work wherever the pointer is, a number box included; after the last patch comes the first again, and with none playing they start at the first or the last.
  • Each time a patch plays (double-click, keys, MIDI or OSC), the list scrolls it to about a third of the way down, its green triangle showing where you are. Scroll freely in between; the list moves only when a patch plays.
  • Drag patches up or down to reorder them (a selected patch takes the rest of the selection with it). + and − in the bottom bar insert and delete patches. In a narrow pane the bar leaves out the playlist's name, then the Full / Geom button.
  • Playing a patch replaces the one showing without asking to save it, so you can move between patches freely; to keep changes, save first (⌘S). File > New, File > Open and Quit still ask.

The Patches panel

View > Patches (⌥⌘6) lists the patches in Documents > Whorld > Patches and its subfolders (the Folder column), to try them out while you build a set. The first time, it opens above the Playlist.

  • Click a patch to play it at once, crossfading as the playlist does (unsaved changes to the patch showing are let go, as when a playlist patch plays). ↑ ↓ move through the list, playing each in turn; typing a name's first letters jumps to it. The green triangle marks the patch playing.
  • Drag patches into the Playlist to add them where you drop them, or right-click and choose Add to Playlist (after the selected entry). ⌘- or ⇧-click to select several.
  • Right-click a patch to Play it, Open as Patch (as File > Open does, so ⌘S saves to it), or Show in Finder.
  • Search narrows the list by name or folder. The folder menu at the top left chooses another folder to list (Choose Folder…), goes back to the Whorld Patches folder, shows it in the Finder, or refreshes the list (it also refreshes whenever you come back to Whorld).

Auto-advance

Auto-advance plays through the playlist on its own.

  • In the Playlist panel's bottom bar, set how long each patch plays, a number and a unit: sec, beats or bars (4 beats). Then click ▶ (or Image > Auto Advance, ⌥⌘L). Click ■ to stop.
  • The patch playing goes on (or the selected one starts, else the first); when its time is up the next one plays, and after the last, the first again. Playing a patch yourself starts its count afresh.
  • Seconds count only while the picture runs, not while paused.
  • Beats and bars follow the music's beat, from an audio input or Ableton Link (the Modulation panel's), whether or not Modulation is on. The switch comes on a beat, or for whole bars on the first beat of a bar.
  • With no beat (no input, not found yet, or lost in a breakdown), the count carries on at the last tempo heard, or 120 BPM, and the line turns orange until the beat is back. The button's tooltip says which.
  • Right-click ▶ (here or in the Perform Bar) to pick a usual length: 15 seconds to 2 minutes, 4 to 16 beats, or 4 to 32 bars.
  • The length and unit are saved with the playlist (Windows Whorld ignores them); a playlist always opens stopped. MIDI and OSC can start and stop it (the Auto Advance target, /w/advance).

Blackout

Image > Blackout (⌘B, or the eye in the Perform Bar) fades the picture to black over a second, and again brings it back. It's black on screen and in Syphon, not in exports, and Whorld goes on running underneath, so the picture comes back where it would have been. MIDI and OSC can do it too (the Blackout target, /w/blackout). Whorld always starts with the picture showing.

Patch mode: Full or Geometry

Whorld 1's patch mode is back. Image > Geometry Only (⌥⌘G) switches between Full and Geometry, and so do the buttons in the Playlist panel's bottom right corner and the Perform Bar, each named after the mode it's in.

  • Full, the usual mode: playing or opening a patch changes everything, as on Windows.
  • Geometry: it changes only the rings' shape and motion: Ring Growth, Ring Spacing, Line Width, Ring Sides, Rotate Speed, Aspect Ratio, Skew Radius, Skew Angle, Star Factor, Pinwheel and the curve and shear rows, each with its waveform, amplitude, frequency and pulse width. The colors (Color Speed, Lightness, Saturation, Alpha and their Bk rows), Hue, the Master controls, the Properties switches and every Global value stay as they are. Change shapes from the playlist without losing the colors and effects you've set.

A patch saved in Geometry mode is saved as you see it. The mode is saved with the playlist (Windows Whorld ignores it), and MIDI or OSC can switch it (the Geometry Mode target, /w/geometry). Switching it from the menu or the button marks the playlist edited; from MIDI or OSC it doesn't.

Full screen and projectors

  • View > Enter Full Screen (⌘F) fills the display the picture is on: the Whorld window's, or the detached picture's window's. ⌘F or Esc leaves.
  • View > Enter Full Screen on Other Display (⇧⌘F, or Option-click the Perform Bar's full-screen button), with more than one display: fills the next display instead, as if you had detached the picture, dragged its window there and pressed ⌘F. The Whorld window stays in view on its own display, with the swipe pad in the picture's place.
  • While full screen, ⇧⌘F (View > Move Full Screen to Next Display) moves the picture on through every display in turn, left to right as arranged in System Settings > Displays. With one display, ⇧⌘F does nothing.
  • However it moved, leaving full screen puts the picture back where it was before: docked, or in its own window where that window was (on the Whorld window's display, if that display is gone). If the other display is disconnected, Whorld leaves full screen.
  • On a second display the menu bar and Dock stay on your main display, so the panels remain usable there. On the main display, full screen hides them (they don't slide in).
  • In full screen the pointer is hidden while it's over the picture. Entering or leaving full screen doesn't move the origin.

Detach and the swipe pad

View > Detach (⌘D) moves the picture into a window of its own, as on Windows, and the panels fill the Whorld window. While Whorld is the active app, the picture's window stays above the Whorld window; with another app active it's an ordinary window. Detach again, or close the picture's window, to put it back. Double-clicking the picture does the same: in the Whorld window it detaches it, and in its own window or on the space it left, it docks it. Full screen ignores double-clicks.

With more than one display, View > Detach to Other Display (⇧⌘D, or Option-click the Perform Bar's Detach button) detaches the picture onto the next display, centered there at its usual size (smaller if that doesn't fit). While detached, ⇧⌘D (View > Move to Next Display) moves its window on through every display in turn, in the same order as ⇧⌘F; ⌘D docks it. If another app is full screen on that display, the window may open in a different Space.

While the picture is detached, its place in the Whorld window becomes a blank swipe pad that works like the picture, so you needn't move the pointer to the other display: swipe to zoom (with ⇧, Damping), hold a Key Map key to change its control, two-finger tap to put a control back, press ⌘← ⌘→ for snapshots, and press and drag to move the origin (the pad stands for the whole picture). The Perform Bar is on the pad then too. Drag the dividers to make the pad smaller.

Changing the picture

The panels

The Whorld window holds the picture in the middle and the panels around it: Parameters, Master, Globals, Properties, Playlist, Patches, Modulation, MIDI Mapping and Key Map. Click the ⓘ by a panel's title for what it controls. Show or hide each from the View menu, or with ⌥⌘ and its number there: ⌥⌘1 Parameters, ⌥⌘2 Master, ⌥⌘3 Globals, ⌥⌘4 Properties, ⌥⌘5 Playlist, ⌥⌘6 Patches, ⌥⌘7 Modulation, ⌥⌘8 MIDI Mapping, ⌥⌘9 Key Map. A panel's shortcut brings it to the front when it's a tab behind another.

  • Panels fit their width: Parameters, Master and Globals never scroll sideways while there is any other way to fit, since a sideways swipe in them moves a slider. As a panel narrows, its sliders first come down to a comfortable length, then the number boxes shrink (showing their values rounded; click one to see and edit the whole value), then the sliders shorten further, and then the columns hide one at a time from the right: in Parameters, Pulse Width, Frequency, Amplitude, Waveform and then Value's box, leaving each parameter's name and slider. Where the Waveform column is narrow it shows the wave's shape instead of its name. A small blue wave at the end of a row, in the shape of its waveform, marks a parameter its oscillator is moving; hover over it for the waveform, amplitude and frequency. Pulse Width is greyed except where the waveform is Pulse, the only one that uses it (it can still be edited). Widen the panel to bring the columns back.
  • Hover over a control's name for a short explanation, starting with its Key Map key if it has one: (S): for Speed. (Settings > General > Show tips on control names turns these off.) Settings > General > Show Key Map keys in panels shows each control's key before its name in Parameters, Master and Globals.
  • Right-click (a two-finger tap, or Control-click) a slider, number box or Waveform menu to set it back to its value in the patch as it was loaded (opened, played from the playlist, or new) or last saved.
  • Keyboard: click a slider and use ← → for fine steps, ⇧← ⇧→ for larger ones, ⇧⌘← ⇧⌘→ to jump to either end. In a number box, ↑ ↓ do the same (with ⇧ larger, with ⇧⌘ to the end of its range, where it has one).
  • Trackpad: rest the pointer on a slider and swipe sideways with two fingers to move it (a tenth of the slider's width covers its range), or on a number box and swipe up or down to step it.
  • Mouse wheel: over a slider each click moves it 1% of its range; over a number box each click is one step (larger with ⇧).
  • Finer or coarser: hold a modifier while swiping or turning the wheel, from finest to coarsest: ⌘ (a slider a tenth as far, a number box in its fine steps), ⌥ (a slider three tenths as far, a number box ten fine steps), none, ⇧ (a slider three times as far, a number box ten larger steps). This works on every panel, the Modulation panel's too.
  • A swipe or turn of the wheel that starts anywhere else scrolls the panel as usual, even as controls pass under the pointer. Pause for half a second before turning the wheel over a control that scrolling brought under the pointer.
  • The Parameters and Globals number boxes show three decimals, so the digits stay put as a value changes; a value with more (typed, or from a patch) shows them all.
  • Properties: click a row, then ↑ ↓ move through the list and Space toggles it. Elsewhere, Space pauses and ⇧Space steps, even with a number box or the playlist selected; in a text field, Space types a space.

Arranging the panels

  • The panels sit in a left, a right and a bottom area; drag the dividers between them to resize.
  • Drag a panel's title bar onto another panel: near one of its edges, it goes above, below, left or right of it; on its title bar or in its middle, the two become tabs, as on Windows. What will happen is highlighted while you drag. Click a tab to show its panel; drag a tab to move that panel out of the stack.
  • To span a whole area (all along the bottom, say, which suits the wide Modulation panel), drop it in the thin strip along that area's outer side: the bottom of the window, just under a bottom panel's title bar for the area's top, or the strip along the picture's edge next to that area; the highlight then runs the area's whole length. Or drop it near the left, right or bottom edge of the picture to add it to that area.
  • Float a panel: drag its title bar anywhere that isn't a dock spot (out of the Whorld window, or onto the middle of the picture) and let go, or double-click its title bar, or use the ⋯ menu on its title bar. Its Dock button, or a double-click on its title bar, docks it again; dragging a floating panel over the Whorld window highlights where it would dock (the window turns see-through to show it).
  • A panel docked again goes back exactly where it was, sizes too, if nothing has moved since; otherwise beside the panel it was next to, or into the same tabs. Floating panels stay above the Whorld windows while Whorld is active, and a panel hidden while floating floats again when shown.
  • From the keyboard: with the pointer over a panel, ⌥⌘ and an arrow moves it a step that way (beside the next panel, or into the next area); with ⇧⌥⌘ all the way along that side. Over a floating panel, ⌥⌘← ⌥⌘→ ⌥⌘↓ dock it on the left, right or bottom. The title bar flashes, also when the panel can go no further. The ⋯ menu lists these moves too.
  • Modulation opens floating; docked in a narrower pane it puts its settings above Hearing (and scrolls, in a short one).
  • Window > Show All Panels shows every panel and fits the window to its screen.

Layouts: Edit, Perform and your own

  • The Window menu lists the layouts, switched with ⌃⌘1, ⌃⌘2 and so on. Edit is the standard arrangement: Parameters on the left beside the picture, and Patches above the Playlist, Properties, Master and Globals along the bottom. Perform is for playing: a large picture with the Perform Bar, Patches above the Playlist on the left, and a narrow Parameters (names and sliders) above Master on the right. The Key Map cheat sheet (`) stands in for the Key Map panel.
  • Each layout remembers how you leave it, like separate workspaces: rearrange Perform, switch to Edit and back, and Perform is as you left it. Each also remembers whether the Perform Bar shows. Switching never docks or detaches the picture, or moves the windows.
  • Window > Reset Layout puts the current layout back to its standard arrangement (yours: as you saved it).
  • Window > Save Layout As… keeps the arrangement now under a name, listed after Edit and Perform with the next ⌃⌘ number. Rename Layout… and Delete Layout… work on your own layouts while they're current.
  • Files: Window > Export Layout… saves the arrangement as a .whlayout file to keep or share; Window > Import Layout… (or double-clicking the file) adds it as a layout named after the file, adapted to the screen. A playlist can carry a layout too: tick Include the panel layout in the Save Playlist As dialog (turn this off in Settings > General > Open playlists with their layout). Windows Whorld ignores it.

Over the picture: swipes, keys and taps

  • Swipe up or down with two fingers (or turn the mouse wheel) to zoom; with ⇧, to change Damping.
  • Press ` (the key left of 1) to show the whole Key Map drawn on a keyboard, over the picture or swipe pad under the pointer, colored by kind (shape, color, Master, hue and origin); press ` again, or Esc, to hide it. While it shows, swipes, held keys, taps and drags go straight through it to the picture, and it follows the Key Map as you change keys. It never shows over a full-screen picture (going full screen or detaching hides it), nor in Syphon or exports; with the picture full screen on another display, use it over the swipe pad.
  • Hold a key while you swipe to change that key's control instead (up increases). As soon as you press the key, the Perform Bar names its control and value, so if it's the wrong key you can let go with nothing changed.
  • Two-finger tap (or right-click) to put a control back to its value when the patch was loaded or last saved: Zoom with nothing held, Damping with ⇧, or the held key's control.
  • Hold the mouse button (or press the trackpad) on the picture and its center follows the mouse; let go and it stays there (Origin in the Properties panel becomes Park, and Edit > Undo puts it back).
  • An off-center origin keeps its place in the picture at any size, so a patch looks the same in the window and full screen. (Windows Whorld keeps the distance from the middle in pixels instead, so there an off-center origin moves toward the middle on a bigger picture.)
  • How far a mouse wheel's click goes is Settings > Input > Wheel sensitivity, and how far a two-finger swipe goes, here and on the panels, is Trackpad sensitivity (×0.25 to ×4; at ×1 a slider's knob moves half as far as your fingers, for fine adjustments).

The Key Map

View > Key Map (⌥⌘9), a panel like the others (it opens floating; dock it beside the picture to keep it in view while you play), lists which key moves which control, and lets you change them. Click a key and press the new one (any plain key but `, which shows the cheat sheet); a key moves one control, and assigning a key that's in use takes it from the other row. Any parameter column (Value, Amplitude, Frequency, Pulse Width, Global), Master row, Hue or the origin can have a key, each with its own speed. The map is a setting, the same for every patch and playlist. File > Key Map > Restore Defaults brings back the starting map, and Export… and Import… keep a map in a file. The starting map:

  • Top letter row, ring shape: Q Ring Spacing, W Line Width, E Ring Growth, R Rings, T Ring Sides, Y Star Factor, U Pinwheel, I Rotate Speed, O Aspect Ratio, P Skew Radius, [ Skew Angle.
  • Home row, color: A Color Speed, S Speed, D Damping, F Fade, G Saturation, H Hue, J Lightness, K Alpha, L Hue Span.
  • Bottom row, Master and background: Z Zoom, X Trail, C Canvas, V Copies, B Tempo, N Spread, M Bk Hue, comma Bk Lightness, period Bk Saturation, slash Bk Alpha.
  • Digits, curves and origin: 1 Even Curve, 2 Odd Curve, 3 Even Shear, 4 Odd Shear, 9 Origin X, 0 Origin Y.

Hue, Rotate Hue and Invert Color

These three color controls from Whorld 1 work again on the Mac (Windows Whorld V2 keeps them in patches but doesn't use them). All three are saved with the patch and can be MIDI mapping targets or set by OSC.

  • Hue (Master panel, 0 to 360 degrees) shifts the color of new rings; existing rings keep theirs, so a change sweeps outward as they're replaced. With Loop Hue on, it shifts the range of colors being looped through.
  • Image > Rotate Hue (⌘7 or F3) turns the colors of the rings already showing by 90 degrees and adds 90 to Hue, so new rings follow; four in a row come back to where you started. Undo puts Hue back, but not the colors already turned.
  • Image > Invert Color (⌘6 or F4, or Properties) shows all rings as a photographic negative: hue and lightness both flip, so while it's on, raising Lightness darkens the rings. The background isn't affected.

Normally a patch's colors carry on from where the previous patch left them, as on Windows, so the same patch can look different each time. To have each patch start from its own Hue, choose Settings > Picture > Patch colors > Start from the patch.

Fading rings

Normally, once there are as many rings as Master > Rings allows, the oldest ring vanishes as each new one appears, and rings that grow past the edge of the picture, or past the canvas (Master > Canvas, around the rings' center), simply leave or vanish. Master > Fade makes them fade out instead: at 20, each ring fades from its own transparency to nothing over its last 20 rings before it would vanish or leave the picture, whichever comes first.

  • That holds however small Canvas is: rings fade out before the canvas removes them. A ring counts as gone once all of it is outside the picture, so with the rings' center off to one side they fade toward the farthest corner.
  • Fade goes from 0 (off, as in Windows Whorld) to 200, is saved with the patch, and can be a MIDI mapping target. Patches with Fade above 0 still open in Windows Whorld, which ignores it.
  • Settings > Picture > Ring fade curve chooses Eased (the default), which starts and ends gently, or Linear.
  • The fade applies to the picture, snapshots, movies and exports; snapshots and movies don't record the Rings setting, so their own ring count stands in for it.
  • With Rings at its maximum (unlimited), rings fade only at the edge of the picture. Rings that shrink toward the center (negative Ring Growth) fade only before the Rings limit.

Audio and tempo

Whorld can move with music. The Audio menu gathers it: modulation on or off, the beat corrections, Ableton Link and Tempo Follows the Beat. The controls are in View > Modulation (⌥⌘7), a panel like the others (it opens floating).

Listening

  • Input: an audio interface, the built-in microphone, or a loopback device such as BlackHole that carries another app's sound. The first time, macOS asks whether Whorld may use audio input. The sound is analyzed as it arrives and never recorded or sent anywhere.
  • Sound from apps (macOS 14.2 and later, in the same menu) listens straight to an app (VLC, Music, Ableton Live, a browser) or to Everything the Mac plays, with no loopback device, and the sound still plays as usual. Apps appear once they've used sound ("quiet" when they aren't playing now); if the app quits, Whorld picks it up again when it plays. The first time, macOS asks whether Whorld may record system audio; if that's refused, Whorld hears only silence, and the status line says where to allow it (System Settings > Privacy & Security > Screen & System Audio Recording). Each new version of Whorld asks again.
  • Channels picks which of the device's inputs to listen to, mixed together.
  • Gain boosts or cuts what Whorld hears. Range sets how far below the top a sound can be and still register (36 dB suits mastered music, which lives in its top 15 to 20 dB; a smaller range makes the music move things more). Auto-level follows the music's recent peak, so quiet and loud material both use the whole range. Hit sensitivity sets how strong a beat must be to count as a hit.
  • Hearing shows what the analysis hears: the overall level; four bands (Bass up to 150 Hz, Low Mid to 600 Hz, High Mid to 4 kHz, Treble above); Brightness (where the sound's energy sits, low to high); and lights for hits: any hit, a Kick (in the bass) or Hats (in the treble), each with how many it has counted a second over the last 4 seconds (with a kick on every beat, Kick times 60 is the tempo).

The beat, bars and phrases

  • Beat: a beat tracker learns the music's tempo from the hits (it shows it once it has the beat, after a few seconds) and keeps a steady beat going on the kick, even where a busy mix hides kicks from the hit detectors, and through a breakdown. It stops a few seconds after the music does. For music with a steady beat, Beat is usually a better source than Kick.
  • Once it has the beat it counts bars (four beats each) and phrases, shown as "bar 5 · beat 2" (bars 1 to 32). Phrase, under the Beat light, shows the bar of an 8-bar phrase, orange on its one.
  • Counting starts on the first beat after the tracker finds the beat, which may not be the music's "one". To line it up, press Reset to 1.1 on the one (a drop, the start of a phrase): the beat nearest your press becomes beat 1 of bar 1, a little early or late alike. Also Audio > Reset to 1.1 (⌘\), a click on the Perform Bar's tempo, or OSC /w/mod/downbeat. A press a little late counts too, and fires the bar and phrase sources at once.
  • − and + beside the meter move the count a beat back or on (with ⇧, a bar): Audio > Beat Back ⌘[, Beat Forward ⌘], Bar Back ⇧⌘[, Bar Forward ⇧⌘].
  • Half Beat Back and Half Beat Forward (⌥⌘[ ⌥⌘], or − and + with ⌥) move the beat itself half a beat later or earlier, twice making a whole beat, for when the tracker has found the off-beats (the Beat light flashes between the kicks). It stays shifted as the tracker follows the music.
  • None of these fires a row on its own, and waves locked to the beat ease to their new place over a beat instead of jumping. Each also has a MIDI target and an OSC address.

The rows: what the sound moves

Each row of the table moves one thing:

  • Source: a level, a band, Brightness, a kind of hit, the beat, or the Beat LFO. With Beats or Bars, a number box appears in front, for a pulse every so many (any number: 1 bar, 16 bars, 3 beats for a polyrhythm, 1/3 beat for triplets), so a row can, say, play the next patch every 16 bars. The Phase menu beside it moves the pulse into each cycle (with 4 bars, 90° is bar 2, 180° bar 3). Older settings' Beat, Bar and 4-Bar … 32-Bar rows open as 1 beat, 1 bar, 4 bars … 32 bars.
  • Target: any parameter's Value, Global or Amplitude column, any Master control, Hue, or the origin. In the Target menu each parameter is a submenu of its columns. An Amplitude target widens (or, negative, narrows) how far the parameter's oscillator swings, by the Amount as a share of its Value slider, so Bass on Star Factor Amplitude makes the stars wobble harder on the bass; it only shows while the oscillator is running (Frequency above 0).
  • Amount: how far the target moves, as a share of its slider, when the source is at its loudest (negative moves it down). Rows on the same target add up.
  • Curve: Linear; Soft (quiet passages count for more); Hard (only loud passages count); Gate (all or nothing above the Threshold).
  • Polarity: Up moves the target from its value upward; Centered moves it either side.
  • Attack and Release (milliseconds) smooth the movement as the sound rises and falls; a hit sends a pulse that fades over its Release.
  • Offset moves a hit or beat row's timing, from −300 to +300 ms. Beat and bar rows can fire early (negative), since the tracker knows where the next beat will fall, so a zoom pulse can lead the kick while a patch change lands just after the drop. A hit (Hit, Kick, Hats) can't be foreseen, so a negative offset does nothing for it.
  • Live shows each row's level as it plays.
  • A row whose Target is an action (Random Phase, Random Origin, Rotate Hue, Clear, Next or Previous Patch, Next or Previous Snapshot) fires it on each hit, or when a sound rises past the Threshold.

The target starts from the patch's own value, and the patch itself never changes: saving keeps its values, it isn't marked Edited, and turning modulation off brings it back exactly. On the panels, while modulation is on, each modulated control's slider shows a second knob outlined in the accent color, where the control is at that moment; its normal knob keeps showing the patch's value.

The number boxes in a row work like the panels': type a value, or step it with ↑ ↓ (larger with ⇧, to either end with ⇧⌘), a two-finger swipe or the mouse wheel; right-click for its starting value. Attack and Release step by 10 ms (100 with ⇧), Offset by 1 ms (10), Threshold by 0.01 (0.1).

Beat LFO and oscillators on the beat

  • Beat LFO (in the Source menu) is a wave locked to the beat, from Link or the music, instead of a sound. Set its length as a number of Beats or Bars, then its Shape (Sine, Triangle, Ramp Up, Ramp Down or Square) and Phase. A cycle starts on the one; Sine and Triangle start halfway up, rising, Ramp Up at the bottom, Ramp Down and Square at the top. It isn't smoothed, so Attack and Release don't apply; its Offset moves it early or late. Without a beat it carries on at the last tempo; before the first beat it stays at 0. With Polarity Centered it swings either side of the patch's value.
  • A parameter's Frequency as the Target (in its submenu, such as Star Factor > Frequency) locks that parameter's oscillator to the beat instead of moving a control. The Source is the cycle's length, from a sixteenth of a beat to 128 bars: whole (3 beats over a 4-beat bar makes a polyrhythm), a fraction such as 1/3 (triplets, exactly) or a decimal. Step it with ↑ ↓, a swipe or the wheel (by one, halving or doubling below one; with ⇧, halving or doubling); right-click for 1. The Phase menu sets where the one falls in the cycle (with a triangle or sine, 0° puts it halfway up, 90° at the top), and Offset moves it slightly, in milliseconds.
  • Only the timing changes: the patch's waveform, amplitude and pulse width are kept, so a parameter whose amplitude is 0 doesn't move (the Amount column says so). If several rows lock the same oscillator, the last one that's on wins. Without a beat, a locked oscillator carries on at the last tempo; turned off, or with Modulation off, it runs at the patch's own frequency again, from where it was.

Modulation on and off, and the starting rows

  • The Modulation switch at the top turns every row off and on (also Audio > Audio Modulation, ⌘', or Mod in the Perform Bar), and Depth scales them all; both work by OSC (/w/mod/on, /w/mod/depth).
  • The rows are a setting, the same for every patch and playlist. Export and Import (top right) keep them in a file; Restore Defaults brings back the starting rows: every beat gives Speed and Line Width a small push, and Star Factor and Pinwheel swell slowly over 4 and 8 bars. Three more start off, to try: Random Phase every 8 bars, Rotate Hue every 16, and Bass widening the lines.
  • The window's sliders (Depth, Gain, Range, Hit sensitivity, each row's Amount) work like the panels'; right-click one for its starting value (Depth 100%, Gain 0 dB, Range 36 dB, Amount 25%).
  • Modulation takes about 20 to 40 milliseconds from a sound to the picture.

Ableton Link

Ableton Link keeps Whorld in time with Ableton Live and other Link apps on this Mac or the local network. Turn it on with the Ableton Link switch.

  • While the session has other apps in it, the beat comes from Link instead of the music: Beat, Bar and the phrases follow the session's tempo, beats and bars exactly, with no time to lock on, and the Beat line shows "Ableton Link", the tempo and where you are.
  • Link doesn't share where phrases start, so press Reset to 1.1 on the one, or move the count by bars with − and + (by bars only: Link sets the beat within the bar). With Start/Stop sync on, the beat where the session starts playing is the one, and the beat stops while the session is stopped.
  • With no other Link apps, Whorld goes back to the beat it hears. If the connection drops, Whorld keeps the beat going at the last tempo for up to 30 seconds and tries to reconnect every 10 seconds ("Reconnecting…"); turning Link off and on again does the same at once.
  • Audio > Tempo Follows the Beat makes Master > Tempo (which times Origin Random) follow the beat's tempo, from Link or the music, without changing the patch.

Keeping it smooth

Listening and analyzing cost almost nothing, but some targets make Whorld draw much more: Ring Spacing moved down, or Rings, Line Width, Copies or Canvas moved up, add rings or make them heavier. On a heavy patch, large amounts on them can drop frames when the music is loud (the Perform Bar shows it). Lower those rows' Amount or the Depth, or move something cheaper: Zoom, Hue, Speed, Star Factor, Pinwheel, the curves and shears, and the origin cost nothing extra.

Audio-reactive video

File > Export > Audio-Reactive Video… makes a video of the current patch moved by a music file, with the music as its sound. Choose the file, then the frame size, frame rate, format, and how much of the file.

  • The Modulation rows (and Depth, Tempo and the audio settings) move the patch as they would live, but frame by frame, so every hit lands on its frame and the same settings make the same video every time.
  • The video starts with the patch's first frame; turn on Run the patch first to let the patch fill the picture for some seconds before it begins.
  • The beat comes from the music, or from a tempo you set. From the music, Whorld looks ahead: beat-driven rows start on the music's first beat, so a quiet intro stays free of them, and that first beat is the one. Mark the downbeat gives the time (seconds into the file) of a "one" instead.
  • Next and Previous Patch step through the playlist; snapshot actions are skipped. You can keep playing while it renders; the progress shows at the bottom of the view, with Cancel.

Files

Patches (.whp), playlists (.whl), snapshots (.whs) and movies (.whm) are the same files Windows Whorld uses, and can be moved between the two in either direction. Double-click one in the Finder to open it, or choose it from File > Open Recent.

The Whorld folder

  • The first time it runs, Whorld makes a Whorld folder in Documents, with Patches, Playlists, Snapshots, Movies, Exports and Layouts folders in it (if you already have a Whorld folder there, they're added and nothing in it moves). Delete one and Whorld won't make it again.
  • Opening or saving a patch starts in Patches, a playlist in Playlists, a snapshot in Snapshots, a movie in Movies, and a layout file in Layouts; after that, each starts where you last opened or saved that kind of file, so patches and playlists stay apart.
  • Patches that come with Whorld are copied into these folders once; a sample you delete isn't copied again.
  • A playlist's patch that isn't where the playlist says is looked for where it was relative to the playlist (so a playlist and its patches can move together, to another folder or another Mac), then beside the playlist, then in Patches. Whorld saves each patch's place relative to the playlist as an extra line Windows Whorld ignores.
  • Missing patches. If a playlist's patches still can't be found when it opens (a playlist from Windows, say, or patches that have moved), Whorld offers to search a folder and its subfolders for them by file name. Patches still missing are dimmed in the Playlist (hover for the path it had). Right-click one and choose Locate… to pick its file; Whorld then looks in that folder for the other missing ones too. Find Missing Patches… in the same menu searches a folder again. Either marks the playlist edited, and Edit > Undo takes it back.

Reopening and saving

  • When you quit, Whorld remembers the playlist and patch you had open (the patch file, or the playlist entry that was playing) and opens them next time; an untitled patch or a never-saved playlist starts fresh, and a file that has since moved is still found. To start with an untitled patch instead, as Windows Whorld does, turn off Settings > General > Reopen the last playlist and patch at launch.
  • A patch can name its author: the title bar shows the playing patch's name and author, and the Playlist has an Author column. Set it in the Save As dialog's Author field (⇧⌘S); for a patch with no author yet it suggests Settings > General > Your name. The author is a line of its own in the patch file, which Windows Whorld ignores (and leaves out if it saves the patch again).

Snapshots

A snapshot (.whs) keeps the picture exactly as it is, every ring. File > Snapshots > Take Snapshot (⌘T) saves one, in Documents > Whorld > Snapshots; Load Snapshot (⌘L) loads one or several. With snapshots loaded, Home, Page Up, Page Down and End show the first, previous, next and last (fn‑← fn‑↑ fn‑↓ fn‑→ on laptops); in full screen or with the pointer over the picture, ⌘← and ⌘→ do too. File > Export > All Snapshots exports each as an image.

Movies, images and video

  • A movie (.whm) is Whorld's own recording of every frame's geometry, so it can be replayed in Whorld and exported at any size. File > Movie > Record Movie… (⌘R) starts one, in Documents > Whorld > Movies, and again stops it (a bar along the bottom of the picture shows it recording); Play Movie… replays one. A movie isn't a video: export it as one.
  • File > Export > Movie as Video… makes a QuickTime video (.mov) as H.264, HEVC, ProRes 422 or ProRes 4444. ProRes 4444 keeps the colors nearly exactly; the others soften color at sharp edges. Movie as Image Sequence exports each frame as an image.
  • File > Export > Image (⌘E) saves the picture as it is now. Images, videos and image sequences go to Documents > Whorld > Exports, or the folder chosen in Settings > Export.
  • Exports are drawn with Metal whenever this Mac can, whatever the Renderer setting says, so they come out the same every time; Intel Macs export with Core Graphics, which is slower with antialiasing on.

Connecting: MIDI, OSC, Syphon

MIDI

Whorld listens to MIDI input through Core MIDI. Choose the input device in Settings. To map a control, open MIDI Mapping (⌥⌘8), select one or more mappings, turn on the panel's Learn switch and move the control on your device.

Besides the targets Windows Whorld has, the Mac adds:

  • Hue, Rotate Hue and Invert Color.
  • For audio modulation: Modulation On, Modulation Depth, Reset to 1.1 (on a press), Beat Back, Beat Forward, Bar Back, Bar Forward and Half Beat (on a press), and Reset Bar Count (on a press: the nearest bar becomes bar 1, the beat kept).
  • Geometry Mode (on is Geometry), Auto Advance (on plays, off stops) and Blackout (on fades to black, off brings it back).

Mappings are saved in the playlist. Those to the Mac's targets go in a section of their own, which Windows Whorld doesn't read, so the playlist still opens there with the rest (saving it on Windows leaves the Mac's out).

OSC and TouchOSC

Everything MIDI can map can also be controlled by OSC over the network. Whorld listens on UDP port 8000 and answers on port 8001 (Settings > Input, which also shows this Mac's address). It sends its state to every device that has sent it something in the last 30 seconds, and to "Also send to" if set: the values of the controls below as they change, and the frame statistics once a second. macOS may ask whether Whorld can use the local network: allow it, or other devices can't reach it.

TouchOSC: the DMG's TouchOSC folder has Whorld.tosc, a layout for a tablet or a computer with four pages: MAIN (Master, the Image switches and actions, the origin), PARAMS, GLOBALS and MONITOR. In TouchOSC set a UDP connection: host = the Whorld Mac's address (127.0.0.1 on the same Mac), send port 8000, receive port 8001; open the layout and start it (▶). It shows "connected" when Whorld answers.

Addresses start with /w. Numbers are what a MIDI mapping sends: 0 to 1 covers a slider's range (values beyond act like a mapping with a wider Start and End). Switches take 1 or 0 (no argument toggles); actions happen on a message with no argument or a nonzero one.

Syphon

Syphon output sends Whorld's picture to other apps on the same Mac, such as Resolume, MadMapper, OBS and TouchDesigner, where it's listed as Whorld.

  • Turn it on with View > Syphon > Syphon Output, or by clicking Syp in the Perform Bar, whose dot shows whether an app is receiving: hollow while none is, filled once one is. It's off until you turn it on.
  • Transparency comes through: where a patch's Bk Alpha or Alpha is below 1, the receiving app can layer Whorld over other material.
  • By default Syphon carries the frames Whorld shows, at their size (the window's, or the display's in full screen, times the Render quality). View > Syphon can send a fixed size instead: 1280 × 720, 1920 × 1080, 3840 × 2160, or Custom Size…; Match the Output goes back. A fixed size is drawn a second time at that size, the picture scaled to fit with more of the pattern on the longer side, and only while an app is receiving it.

Settings and performance

Whorld > Settings (⌘,) holds the options, in tabs by task, each section saying what it's for:

  • General: starting up and updates; your name, playlists' layouts and the Whorld folder; help in the panels.
  • Picture: patch colors, the ring fade curve, and the frame rate, which sets how fast patches run.
  • Performance: the renderer, Render quality, Upscaling and Curve quality.
  • Input: trackpad and mouse sensitivity, the MIDI input, and OSC.
  • Export: images and movies, and snapshots.

For speed:

  • Renderer (Settings > Performance): Metal (the GPU, the default) or Core Graphics (the CPU). Metal needs an Apple silicon Mac; on an Intel Mac, or if Metal fails, Whorld draws with Core Graphics.
  • Frame rate (Settings > Picture) can hold the picture to 60 fps. Rings move a step each frame, so on a 120 Hz display patches otherwise run twice as fast as on a 60 Hz monitor; 60 fps also uses less power.
  • Render quality (the Perform Bar's Res menu, or Settings > Performance) trades sharpness for speed: at 75%, 50% or 33% the picture is drawn with fewer pixels and scaled up. Rings and lines keep their size; only edges get coarser. At 50% the graphics work is roughly a third to a half of full resolution's, so a patch that drops frames may run smoothly again (work that doesn't depend on pixels, such as building very complex rings, doesn't shrink). Exports aren't affected; Syphon gets the smaller frames unless its size is fixed.
  • Upscaling chooses how it's enlarged: Smooth, or Sharp pixels, which keeps the pixels square and crisp (most even at 50%) and suits patches drawn without antialiasing.
  • Curve quality (Settings > Performance, Low to High) helps where Render quality can't, on patches with large curved rings such as bloom, typhoon and boomer: curves are drawn as short straight segments, and lower quality uses fewer (boomer at full screen with antialiasing: about 54 fps at High, 69 at Low on an M1 Max). At Low, curves may look faceted by up to a pixel; straight-sided patches look the same at any setting. High is the default and how exports are always drawn.
  • Automatic quality (View > Automatic Quality, ⌘;, or Auto in the Perform Bar) does this for you: when the picture can't keep up, Whorld lowers Curve quality first, then Render quality to 75% and 50%, a step at a time, and raises them again once there's room, never above your settings. The Perform Bar shows Auto ↓ in orange, its tooltip saying what's lowered (for example, "curves 1.0 px, 75%"). On an M1 Max at 120 Hz full screen, boomer settles at 75% and runs at a steady 120 fps; a change of scale costs one frame.

Reference

Updates

Once a day Whorld asks GitHub whether a newer version has been released (only Whorld's version number is sent). If there is one, it shows what's new, with a button that opens the download page; install it as before, by dragging Whorld from the DMG to Applications. If you're running a beta, you're offered new betas too. Nothing is announced while Whorld is full screen. Choose Whorld > Check for Updates to check now, or turn off automatic checks in Settings > General.

Bugs and ideas

Help > Report a Bug and Help > Request a Feature open a form on GitHub, with your Whorld version, macOS version and Mac filled in. You need a free GitHub account to send it. Requests from others are on the feature requests page; a 👍 on one helps decide what comes next.

First launch

This build is not notarized by Apple, so the first time you open it macOS says it can't check the app for malicious software. Control-click Whorld in the Applications folder, choose Open, then Open again. On macOS 15 and later, if there is no Open button, go to System Settings > Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. You only need to do this once.
